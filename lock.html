LOCK

Physical Computing · Objectified [dis]Topias, sophomore spring · Solo project

Overview

A motorized door lock built from exposed parts. Press a button and an Arduino drives a 12V linear actuator, which pushes a lever I made from aluminum and slides the bolt of a keyed barrel lock open or closed. It hangs on my bedroom door, and every wire and board is left on display.

Concept

The assignment for Objectified [dis]Topias was to design for a dystopia of any kind. I researched a world where security is heavily monitored, where every lock is connected and every door reports back to someone.

My answer was to go the other way: an analog, vintage-looking lock that you can see working. Nothing is hidden in a housing or an app. The circuit, the motor driver and the actuator are all mounted in the open, so you can follow exactly what happens when the button is pressed.

It was also my chance to learn Arduino properly. This was the project where I learned to write Arduino code and to drive a motor that needs more power than the board can give on its own.

Build

Everything mounts to a single metal plate. The barrel lock sits at the bottom, with the actuator above it. I cut, bent and drilled a strip of aluminum into a lever that links the actuator's rod to the bolt, so when the rod extends or retracts, the bolt slides with it.

My teacher suggested leaning into the exposed look for the controls too, so the push buttons run off on very long wires instead of being tucked away. The wiring on the plate is routed by hand in straight runs, so the electronics read as part of the design.

Circuit

The Arduino can't power a 12V actuator by itself, so it goes through an L298N motor driver. The Arduino reads the button and sends a direction signal to the driver, and the driver switches the 12V supply to push the actuator out or pull it back in. One power jack feeds the driver, and the driver feeds the actuator.

Reflection

This project taught me how to work with Arduino and how to power and control a motor safely through a driver. It also showed me how much of a project's meaning can come from what you choose to show. Leaving the wiring exposed turned a lock into a statement about trust and visibility.

The next step I wanted to explore, and didn't have time for, was swapping the wired buttons for a Bluetooth connection, and thinking about how to keep that wireless version as transparent as the wired one.

Technologies Used

  • Arduino Uno
  • L298N motor driver
  • 12V linear actuator
  • Keyed barrel lock
  • Hand-cut aluminum lever and mounting plate
